# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A long-tenured viewer who cancelled his subscription after the catalog stopped serving his evenings and now relies on an outside source to tell him what to watch.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To spend his evening watching something genuinely good without having to sift through a huge library to find it.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Tom describes StreamLine as having become “a warehouse”: volume went up while the quality of his evenings went down. He cancelled, and now a competitor emailing him two hand-picked films a week gets both watched. This is the hook made literal: the curation happens elsewhere, and so does his money.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** A competitor’s curated email newsletter (two hand-picked films a week)
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Observed (from the interview):

Receives the competitor’s email with two hand-picked films each week.
Watches both films.

Implied by his story (inferred, not stated):
3. Accesses those films through the competitor’s service or another source. The interview doesn’t say which; what we know is that it isn’t StreamLine, since he cancelled.
4. Returns to the email the following week for the next pair, with no need to browse anything himself.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** It is efficient for Tom, which is the problem. The inefficiency sits on StreamLine’s side and in the broader system:

Fragmented path from “what to watch” to “watching it.” Curation lives in one place and the viewing happens in another. Even for Tom, it depends on a third party’s picks and on the film being available wherever he watches.
Narrow supply. Two films a week is a small, passive feed. It works because it removes choice, which is also its ceiling: Tom has traded breadth for certainty.
No personalization signal. Nothing in the interview suggests the email knows his taste. He trusts it because someone selected the films, not because it learned him. The curation is human, but it is generic.
The research doesn’t show Tom’s costs. He reports no frustration with the workaround. Its inefficiency is a business problem, not a user pain point, and it’s worth being honest about that in the persona.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** I trust a recommendation from a friend more than the algorithm. The algorithm just wants me to keep scrolling, not to actually find something good
